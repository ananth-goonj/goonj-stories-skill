# The method in plain words

This review reads a folder of field stories and answers six questions about it: what the work looks like (Q1), whether places repeat (Q2), whether one village's work led another to ask (Q3), what people felt and whether it is in their own words (Q4), why people chose the work (Q5), and what the stories leave out (Q6). It also scores how ready each story is for outside use, lists who must agree before a name or detail goes outside, and writes questions for each writer.

## The steps
1. **Read.** A script turns each story file into plain text (body, headers, footers, text boxes). The source files are never changed.
2. **Extract.** One agent per story (or per few stories in a big batch) fills a fixed record from the codebook: about sixty fields plus the 19 Key Questions. Every coded value carries an exact quote from the story as evidence.
3. **Gate.** A script checks every record: allowed values, dates and counts, that every evidence quote is an exact copy of the story text, that fields agree with each other, and that sensitive words in the text are covered by a sensitivity flag.
4. **Second read (Full only).** A different agent reads each story again and corrects the record. Every correction is logged with its reason.
5. **Batch rulings (Full only).** A script lists places where the same kind of fact was coded two ways; one reader settles each with a written rule that is applied to every story it covers.
6. **Compute.** Scripts derive flags and group roll-ups, count everything, search for focus words in and out of quotations, and score readiness with a fixed 12-test checklist.
7. **Findings (Full only).** Analysts write findings as templates. A script fills in every number and story id from a query and rejects any number typed by hand. Three independent skeptics (evidence, confounds, fairness) test each finding; a fixed rule drops the ones they refute; an editor writes the final wording, and the script checks it again.
8. **Build and check.** A report, a workbook and per-story readings are built, then checked: formulas recounted, no web address or script in the report, no withheld name anywhere.

## What the scripts guarantee, and what they do not
- **Evidence is an exact copy.** A quote in a record is a real piece of the story. This does not prove the code is right; second readers and skeptics exist for that.
- **Numbers are computed.** No count in a table, chart or finding was typed by a person or an agent. A correct count over a wrong question is still wrong, so skeptics keep their job.
- **Privacy.** Names tied to a health, injury, bereavement or menstrual detail are withheld everywhere. The consent register shows how personal a detail is and where it sits, never what it is, story by story. No output ranks writers or scores a story by how many questions it answers.

## What the Quick check leaves out
No second read, no batch rulings, no feelings or framing coding, and no written findings. It still gives the 19 Key Question coverage, readiness bands, the consent register, the counts, and the questions for each writer. A Quick check can be upgraded to Full later without reading the stories again.

## Limits
The stories are self-reports by field staff. Counts describe the batch, not the programme or the place. Absence in a story is absence in the write-up. Small batches show observations, not trends.
