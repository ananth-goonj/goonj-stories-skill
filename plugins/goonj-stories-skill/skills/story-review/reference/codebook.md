# Story extraction codebook

You are turning ONE Goonj field story into ONE JSON record. The record is evidence for an internal learning review, so it must say only what the story text says. A script (the gate) checks your record, and a second reader may check it again. Read this whole file before the first story; it settles the questions earlier extractors raised, so that every story is coded the same way.

## 0. Golden rules
1. **The text is the only evidence.** Every value comes from the story file. `null` means "not in the text". Never fill from plausibility, another story, a surname, a template default, or anything you know about the place, the programmes, local units or crops.
2. **Verbatim evidence.** Every judgement field has an `evidence` (or `*_evidence`) string that is an EXACT copy of a span of the story text (the language as written, at most 300 characters, no ellipsis inside, no paraphrase, no translation). The script compares character by character after it collapses spaces and straightens quotes and dashes. If you cannot point to text, the value is `null` / `"none"` / `"not_stated"` and the evidence is `""`.
3. **Declared inference only.** If you infer (a state from a district, a year from other dates), say so in the field's `basis`, or in `date_issues` / `location_conflicts` with the word "inferred".
4. **Both values, never the better one.** If a header, footer and body disagree, record the structured (labelled) value and write both, verbatim, in the matching `*_issues` or `*_conflicts` field.
5. **Computed is not stated.** Sums, day counts from a date range and inferred zeros are labelled `computed` or `inferred`.
6. **Quotes are the writer's rendering.** In a story that is a translation, every quote is the translator's rendering. Record exactly what is written and never present reported speech as a quote.
7. **No identity from names.** Never infer caste, religion or tribe from a surname or a hamlet name. Record a community label only where the text uses the word, at settlement level.
8. **Read the whole file** before writing: the `#` header lines, the body, and every header or footer line (codes, writer, labelled places).
9. **Numbers in your own sentences** (answers, summaries, learnings, missing, questions) must come from the story text. Copy a number as the story writes it; do not add up, convert or round.

## 1. What you are given
- `stories/<ID>.txt`: a few `#` lines written by a script (id, source file, images, notes), then the text after `=== body ===`. Footer lines such as "Village:", "Block:", "Participants:", "Report Code:", "Written by" are part of the text and are evidence.
- `prefill/<ID>.json`: candidates a script found (codes, credits, labelled places, date strings). Use it as a checklist, not as truth.
- `record_template.json`: the exact shape of your record. The one-item lists in it only show the shape of an entry; replace them with your entries or leave the list empty (`[]`).
- This codebook, with the allowed values and the 19 Key Questions generated below. Use only those strings.
- A `# NOTE` line may say the story is a translation, or that the text looks like a legacy Hindi font (garbled letters). If it is garbled, code only what is readable and say so in `_notes`.

## 2. Record structure

### 2.1 Identity and provenance
- `story_id`, `source_file`, `source_folder`: copy from the header. `title`: the story's own title line, or `null`.
- `language`: `hindi` | `english` | `mixed` (English metadata over a Hindi body is `hindi`).
- `translated`: the batch setting is in the header or your brief; if it says translated, set `true`, and write one line in `translation_note` ("English translation; original language not supplied"). For a translation, copy each quote into both `quote_original` and `quote_english`. For a story in its own language, `quote_original` is the words as written and `quote_english` is your translation.
- `register`: `field_report` (plain chronological account with names, dates, numbers) | `feature_narrative` (a headed account with sub-headings and quotes, written like a feature) | `structured_template` (a form with labelled fields) | `literary_narrative` (figurative, generic, few facts) | `testimonial` (one person's life story) | `social_media_post` | `mixed`. A short plain account without names or dates is still `field_report`.
- `report_codes`, `activity_codes`: every code exactly as written. A- codes and anything labelled "Activity Code" go in `activity_codes`; D- codes, bare numbers and "D:" go in `report_codes`. A bare code line goes by its prefix. A code in a different format ("D- 23957", a bare number, "D: 80556") is flagged by the script, not by you.
- `writer`, `verifier`: verbatim (single names are normal). `other_credits`: any other named role. `null` if absent.

### 2.2 Programmes (from the text, never from the file's folder)
`programmes`: one entry for each programme the text shows. `named_in_text` is true only when the programme's name or its kit name is written (CFW / Cloth for Work / सीएफडब्ल्यू; NJPC / चुप्पी तोड़ो / My Pad / Women Kit; S2S / school kit; Rahat as relief). Menstrual-topic words alone mean `NJPC` with `named_in_text: false`. Shramdaan plus a family or relief kit with no CFW word means `CFW`, `named_in_text: false`. A garden that grows out of a women's health or nutrition meeting, with no menstrual content and no NJPC kit, is `Kitchen_garden_initiative`, `named_in_text: false`. Individual-support stories with no programme word use the programme the batch brief names, `named_in_text: false`; do not expand its meaning. If nothing in the text names or shows a programme, use `not_stated` with `named_in_text: false` and empty evidence. A label line that names a programme the content does not show is not coded; add the flag `label_programme_not_in_content`.
`partner_org`: a named partner organisation, or `null`.

### 2.3 Location (where the activity happened)
`location` has one object for each of `state`, `district`, `block`, `panchayat`, `village`, `tola`, `ward`: `value` (as written, original script, without the label word), `basis` (`stated_body`, `stated_footer` for a labelled line anywhere, `inferred_from_block`, `inferred_from_district`, `inferred_from_team`, `inferred_from_folder`, `none`) and `evidence`.
- The place is where the work happened: for a meeting its venue, for a livelihood centre the centre, for individual support the person's village. A different place of residence is not recorded in `location`.
- A state the text never writes stays `null` (basis `none`). Do not work it out from a district or from anything you know. Use `inferred_from_folder` only if the header NOTE or your prompt tells you the state of the batch.
- Take `block` from the word "Block" in the text. "Police Station: X" is not the block. If one place is called a block in one place and a police station in another, set the flag `place_type_variant`. A "Gram Panchayat" goes in `panchayat`. Compound names ("Name Musahari Tola") stay whole in `village` unless the text separates them. A village known only from a school name is allowed; say so in `_notes`.
- `location_conflicts`: verbatim, when the text places the site in two districts or blocks.
- `households_or_families`: the resident count as stated, with evidence. Counts of people who used or received something belong in `participants.issues` or `outcomes`.
- `community_labels`: only words the text uses: `label_verbatim`, `level` (`settlement_name` for a word inside a place name, `community` for a word describing people such as "Adivasi families" or "Hindu, Muslim and Adivasi communities"), `evidence`. Occupation words (labourers) are not community labels. Add `identity_label` to `sensitivity.flags` for a `community` label.

### 2.4 Activities and why they were chosen
`activities`: one entry for each distinct work in the story; the first is the primary one (the work in the title or with most of the narrative). A work mentioned in one passing clause is still listed, with `detail` saying "mentioned in passing". `activity_primary` is the first entry's code.
- `code`: the closest allowed activity; `other` plus `detail` if none fits. What the codes mean:
  - access: `road_repair` (repairing, levelling or earth-filling an existing road or path) · `road_new_or_widened` (making a kachcha road where there was a footpath, widening a path) · `roadside_bush_clearing` · `road_cleaning` (clearing an existing or old road of weeds and debris) · `bamboo_bridge` · `bridge_approach_or_repair` · `culvert_or_causeway` · `approach_road_to_water_point` (a road or path built to reach a tubewell, well or pond; not a road that merely passes one)
  - water: `canal_cleaning` (silt or weeds) · `canal_or_channel_digging` (a new channel, widening a river mouth) · `drain_nala_cleaning` · `pond_cleaning` · `pond_side_clearing` (bushes round a pond, not the pond itself) · `well_cleaning` · `check_dam_repair` · `check_dam_construction` · `embankment_repair` · `embankment_construction` (a new raised embankment) · `handpump_premises_cleaning` · `handpump_enclosure` (a fence or screen round a handpump) · `drainage_outlet_construction` (a new outlet, pipe or channel to drain water away) · `river_or_waterbody_clearing` · `ghat_cleaning`
  - community space: `chabutra_construction` · `chabutra_repair` · `community_place_cleaning` · `community_shelter` (a shed, sitting place or roof of tarpaulin, bamboo or straw) · `community_space_repair` (repair of a community building, shed or place) · `religious_site_cleaning` · `boundary_wall_or_fencing` · `ground_levelling` · `raised_platform_or_refuge` (high ground for flood refuge)
  - hygiene: `village_cleanliness_drive` · `school_campus_cleaning` · `school_or_anganwadi_repair` · `toilet_construction` · `bathing_enclosure`
  - health, nutrition, farming: `mh_awareness_meeting` · `kitchen_garden` · `community_farm` (a shared plot of food crops) · `women_kit_distribution` (a women's kit or pads given)
  - school: `school_kit_distribution` (bags, kits, bottles, a game box) · `school_material_support` (mats, other items) · `school_structure` (shade or shelter for a school)
  - relief and individual: `flood_relief_kit` · `winter_relief` (blankets) · `individual_support_kit` (groceries, a marriage kit, shop material) · `livelihood_work_at_centre`
  `detail`: one sentence using the text's own verbs (repair is not widening; cleaning is not construction). `dimensions_verbatim`: length, breadth, height or area exactly as written, with units, or `null`. Do not convert units. If the text gives the size in two forms, put both, joined with "; ", and set `two_size_figures`.
- **Reason** (code this carefully): `reason_type` is `problem` (a stated difficulty, lack, hazard or damage), `purpose` (only an aim or benefit: "ताकि...", "जिससे...", "so that..."), `works_named_as_needs` (works are listed as "problems" but no difficulty is described) or `none`. `need_verbatim` holds the reason span, a problem if there is one, else the purpose; `""` if none. With `works_named_as_needs` or `none`, `need_choice_link` is `absent`.
- `need_voiced_by`: who says the problem. If the writer describes it but says villagers raised or discussed it ("ग्रामीणों ने कई बार बैठकों में चर्चा की", "people said that..."), use `villagers_collective` with `need_speech_type: writer_narration` (or `collective` / `reported` if their words are given). A condition simply described is `writer_narration`. A team's purpose line is `goonj_staff`. `need_speech_type`: `direct_quote`, `direct_unmarked` (first-person words with no quote marks, including "X said that we ..." where the content is first person), `reported` (third person), `collective`, `writer_narration`, `none`.
- `need_timing`: `before_decision` when the need is framed as the reason for the decision, even if written later ("while seeking a solution to this problem... decided"); `post_hoc_only` when it appears only in describing benefits afterwards.
- `prior_attempts`: any statement that people tried, asked, or long wanted to do it and could not ("told the block many times", "had been postponing for years"), else `null`. Officials ignoring people is a prior attempt, not a feeling, unless a feeling word is written.
- `proposer`: who proposed the work. A named villager who starts the effort is the proposer even if the group then picks the works. Passive verbs whose only possible agent is the team in a team-run session ("women were encouraged...", "women were asked...") are `goonj_staff`. "A suggestion was made" or "it was decided" with no agent and no context is `unattributed_passive`. A local volunteer is `volunteer` (also `trigger: volunteer_or_local_referral`). `proposer_evidence` is required for `named_villager`.
- `trigger`: `goonj_meeting`, `saw_work_elsewhere`, `villager_request`, `volunteer_or_local_referral`, `partner_request`, `found_during_other_goonj_work`, `programme_menu` (the activity is offered by the programme, such as a kitchen garden in every health meeting), `disaster_event`, `not_stated`. When Goonj is never named and no convener is stated: `trigger: not_stated`.
- `options_considered`: verbatim if the text shows people listing or ranking options ("first those works that benefit the whole village"), else `null`.
- `decision_depiction`: `narrated` = the text shows how the choice was made: a named or role-identified person proposes, persuades or decides, OR the content of the deliberation is given (reasons weighed, options ranked). `asserted` = a collective formula, even with a reason clause or "unanimously": "everyone decided together", "सभी ने मिलकर निर्णय लिया". `staff_decision` = Goonj staff decided. `none`. `decision_evidence` is required for `narrated`.
- `need_choice_link`: `explicit` only with a causal connector or explicit reference ("because", "so that", "therefore", "in view of this need", "इस समस्या", "इसलिए", "ताकि", "समाधान के लिए"); a link only in time ("after the discussion") is `implicit`; no need is `absent`; the stated need and the work do not match is `mismatch`. `link_note`: one line.
- `agenda_source`: `community`, `programme_menu` (unless the text shows the group choosing it for a stated need: then `mixed`), `mixed`, `unclear`.
- Several works under one decision: secondary works inherit the campaign's `proposer`, `trigger`, `decision_depiction` and `agenda_source`; give them a `need_verbatim` only if the text states a need for that work, otherwise `reason_type: none` and `need_choice_link: absent`.
- A kit that is the only stated need (families lacked clothes; the kit answers it): the works get `reason_type: none`, and the flag `kit_as_motive`.
- `purposes` (whole story, only what the text names): from the allowed list.

### 2.5 Time
`dates`: every date or range in the text. `type`: `work_start`, `work_end`, `work_range`, `meeting`, `distribution`, `event`, `other`. `verbatim`; `iso` (`YYYY-MM-DD`, or `YYYY-MM` with no day, `null` with no year and none inferable); `iso_end` (end of a range, else null); `precision`; `year_inferred` (true if the year is not written: use the year the same story gives for the work, and say "inferred from the ... dates in the same story" in `date_issues`); `typo_fixed`. "Between 4 and 8 September 2026" is `work_range`, `iso: 2026-09-04`, `iso_end: 2026-09-08`. A two-digit year ("26") is not a typo (note it in `date_issues`).
`working_days`: `value` only from a stated number ("three days", "five days"), never computed from a range; `basis` `stated` or `none`; `evidence`. A range much longer than the stated days is not a conflict unless the text says "continuously" or equates the range with the days; then add `date_conflict`. `date_issues`: contradictions, verbatim.
`timeline`: `work_month` (YYYY-MM of the work start: from a work date; else a meeting or distribution date, saying which in `work_month_basis`; else `null`).

### 2.6 Participation
`participants`: `total`, `male`, `female` (integers or `null`), `basis` (`stated`, `computed_total`, `women_only_count`, `approximate`, `meeting_attendance`, `none`), `evidence`, `issues`.
- "24 women worked" is a women-only count: `female` = 24, `total: null`, `basis: women_only_count`, explained in `issues`. A total of "men and women" with no split: `total` = that number, `male` and `female` null, `basis: stated`. No count: all null, `basis: none`.
- Meeting attendance ("40-50 women came to the meeting") is `meeting_attendance` only when the primary activity is a meeting: `total` = the lower number of a range, `female` = total if only women attended, the verbatim range in `issues`. It is never added to work participants, and it is not work participation unless the text says they did the work.
- A participant list that is not a count of workers is never added. A women-only count is a minimum, not a total. Never infer sex from names.

### 2.7 Contributions, upkeep, how Goonj came in
- `contributions`: one entry for every contribution the text names from the community or others: `type` (cash, materials, tools, labour, transport, land_or_site, expertise), `who` (as written; **a contribution needs a stated giver, otherwise leave it out**), `detail` (your short line), `amount_text` (exactly as written, else null), `evidence`. Labour counts only when the text says people gave their own labour.
- `maintenance_or_ownership`: `status` `pledge` (people say they will look after it: future tense or intention), `arrangement_described` (the text says who does it and how, or names a body given the job), `asserted_responsibility` (the community has taken responsibility, no detail), `appeal_to_authority` (people ask the government or administration to take over or fix it), `none_stated` (default; empty evidence). `who` as written. Give the stronger one if two apply. "Villagers expressed willingness to continue working" is not upkeep; "we will also try to maintain what we have built" is a `pledge`.

- `prior_contact`: every statement of earlier or outside contact: `type` (earlier_goonj_work_here, saw_work_in_neighbouring_place, volunteer_or_local_referral, found_during_other_goonj_work, knew_goonj, relative_or_other_source, other), `source_place_verbatim`, `evidence`. "Neighbours praised it" is not adoption; a volunteer referral is outreach, not spread.
<!-- /FULL -->
- `entry_point`: how Goonj came in (the allowed list). **`entry_convener_stated`**: true only if the text says who convened or held the meeting ("a meeting held by Goonj", "the Goonj team called a meeting"). Naming Goonj staff as present is not enough. It can be true only when `goonj_named` is true. `entry_point_detail`: one line. When a meeting has no stated convener and the text never names Goonj, the team or the organisation: `entry_point: unclear`, `trigger: not_stated` (the script derives the flag `goonj_absent`). "The team" or "the organisation" referring to Goonj counts as Goonj.
- `facilitation_depth` describes how the Goonj team ran the session, so it is `not_described` whenever Goonj or its team is not named as taking part. Otherwise: `not_described` (nothing about what the team did); `standard_meeting` (a meeting or explanation, then villagers chose); `substantive` (the method is described: what the team did or held back, how the discussion moved). `facilitation_evidence`.
- `goonj_named`: true only when "Goonj" is written or a person is described as Goonj's ("X from Goonj", "a Goonj representative"). "CFW" alone makes the programme named and leaves `goonj_named` false. `staff_named`: names of Goonj staff or volunteers shown acting, verbatim, or `null`.
- `kit`: `mentioned`; `kit_names` as written (an empty list when "materials" or "support" are mentioned without a kit or contents: then `mentioned: true`, `framing: given_unframed`, evidence = the sentence); `framing`: `given_unframed` (given with no framing word: "was given", "provided", "distributed"); `gift_or_reward` ONLY with explicit words (gift, reward, उपहार, प्रोत्साहन स्वरूप, इनाम); `exchange_for_work` ("in return for this work", "श्रमदान के बदले"); `received_with_dignity` ("सम्मानस्वरूप"); `materials_for_work`; `support_to_individual`; `not_mentioned`.

### 2.8 Voices
`quotes`: every passage presented as someone's words: `speaker` (as written or null), `role` (only what the text says: "a doctor from the village", "head teacher"; never default to "villager"), `age` (integer or null), `gender` (only from the text's own grammar, title or kinship words such as देवी, कुमारी, श्री, भाभी, "she said", never from a first name; otherwise `unknown`), `is_beneficiary`, `speech_type`, `quote_original`, `quote_english`, `sensitive`.
- `is_beneficiary`: true for residents of the place where the work or support happened (even if not shown working, including a village doctor) and for people who received support; false for office-holders (sarpanch, mukhiya, ward member, headmaster, teacher), passers-by, people from other villages and staff; null when unclear.
- `quote_original` is the whole passage, exact, even when it is longer than 300 characters (the 300-character limit is for evidence spans only). Do not cut a quotation short.
- Record reported speech as well, one entry for each statement: a group's "many women said that they ..." is a `quotes` entry with `speech_type: reported` (or `collective` when the words are in quote marks), with `speaker` null and `role` as the text puts it ("women of the village"). Counts of voices and the flag for stories that only report speech depend on this, so keep one rule across all stories.
- `speech_type`: `direct_marked` (in quote marks), `direct_unmarked` (first-person words without marks, including "X said that we ..."), `reported` (third person), `collective` (group-attributed words in quote marks). Marks around a title, organisation, programme or proverb are not speech.
- `sensitive: true` for a named person's menstrual or health disclosure, a named minor, begging or leprosy, widowhood, disability, or a named person describing a family death.
<!-- FULL -->
`emotions`: one entry for each feeling the text attributes to someone (a problem such as hardship as a noun is a need, not a feeling): `verbatim` (exact span), `english`, `class`, `valence`, `phase` (`before` the work or meeting began; `during` from the decision to the end of the work; `after` completion; `anticipated` future), `holder` (as written), `holder_is_beneficiary`, `voice`, `anchor`, `boilerplate`.
- A span with two feelings gives two entries. Manner tags on speech ("said, smiling") are `writer_observed`. Thanks followed by the person's words take that speech type; "expressed gratitude" with no words is `writer_asserted`. A group's wordless act is `writer_asserted`; `collective` needs words attributed to the group. "Distressed" as an adjective for people is `hardship_helplessness`, phase `before`. Fear of snakes is `fear_anxiety` anchored to `hazard`. Words such as self-reliant, empowered, "an example of unity" are the writer's framing (record under `framing.agency_language`), not feelings, unless a person says they feel it. A closing thanks is `gratitude` with `boilerplate: true`.
`feelings_summary`: `before` and `after` (one line each: the feeling and in whose voice, or "not described"), `same_holder_shift` (true if the same named person or group is shown feeling one way before and another after).
`hesitation_or_friction`: exact spans showing doubt, reluctance, objection or conflict (a landowner closing a path, women hesitating to speak). `[]` if none. Tie it to a feeling with `anchor: social_relations`.

### 2.9 Outcomes
`outcomes`: `immediate` (what the work produced at completion), `anticipated` (future or conditional), `observed_later` (each `{text, time_marker}`; only with an explicit time gap or a later event: "a few days later", "after two days the river flooded and the water did not enter", "recently... was made concrete", "about a year and a half later"; copy `time_marker` exactly). "Now..." alone is `immediate` for an immediate asset benefit; for benefits that need time (harvest, income, others copying) with no time gap, use `anticipated` and add `outcome_asserted_unobserved`. An estimate stays an estimate: write "estimated" in the outcome text. Outcome texts may be in English.
<!-- FULL -->
`gratitude_credit_dependency_agency`: entries `{class, voice, evidence}`: `dependency` needs an explicit counterfactual about Goonj ("without Goonj this would not have happened", "keep giving"); completion credited to Goonj's support or guidance with no counterfactual is `credit`; thanks is `gratitude`; stated self-reliance is `agency`.
`framing`: `deficit_language` (exact spans that describe people mainly by lack or pity) and `agency_language` (exact spans that frame people as actors).
<!-- /FULL -->

### 2.10 Sensitivity and flags
- `sensitivity.flags`: from the allowed list, `["none"]` if nothing applies. `sensitivity.notes`: a short note. **`sensitivity.named_people`: list, as written, every person tied to a sensitivity flag (a named woman with a menstrual or health detail, a named minor, a named person describing a family death, and so on) and every speaker of a quote marked `sensitive`.** The script uses this list to withhold those names everywhere in the outputs. If the story text has menstrual, health, injury or bereavement words and you set the flags to `none`, say in `sensitivity.notes` why it is not personal.
- `internal_flags`: only the judgement flags listed under "internal_flags you may set" below, plus `other:<short text>`. The script derives the rest from your fields (listed as never to set); setting one is an error.
- `claim_from_memory`: a headline claim that rests on what people recall ("nearly 150 years"), not on a record. `possible_translation_slip`: a word looks like a translation or typing slip (for example "mutation garden" beside "nutrition garden"); quote it as written in any evidence span, do not correct it, and say in `_notes` what the likely word is, labelled as an inference.

### 2.11 The 19 Key Questions
Fill `key_questions` for every question in the generated section below. The answer is what the story says, in one or two plain sentences, with up to 3 exact spans as evidence. Do not turn a thin answer into a clear one: a stock phrase is `partial`. Where a question rests on a field you already coded (participants, dates, location, kit, upkeep), the two must agree: the gate rejects `q06` that says a split is given when `participants` has no women/men pair (and the tag `no_split_given` must be present exactly when it has none), and `q19` that says nothing is stated when `maintenance_or_ownership.status` is not `none_stated`.

### 2.12 Reading fields
- `one_line_summary`: one English sentence, facts only.
- `learnings`: 2 to 4 short sentences, each one thing this story is actually telling an internal reader: what the problem was, why people chose the work, who drove it, what it changed, what it shows about how Goonj's approach played out here. Ground every sentence in the text; no outside knowledge, no claim about villagers the text does not show, no blame. They are your reading, so they need no evidence spans.
- `missing`: specific things a reader would need that the story does not give, one short line each ("no dates", "no road length", "no split of the 24 by sex").
- `use_note`: one sentence: what an internal reader can use this story for now, and what is needed before outside use.
- `writer_questions`: 1 to 5, each `{priority, question, why}`: `P1` a conflict or unclear statement to resolve (dates, counts, places, a programme label), `P2` a missing fact (district, date, women/men split, code), `P3` a follow-up worth a call (what happened after, the present state of the asset, whether a named person consented). Plain English, neutral, specific, no blame ("Which block is X in?", not "Why was the block wrong?"). Name templates and processes, never people, as causes.
- `_confidence`: `high` | `medium` | `low`. `_notes`: anything a second reader should look at.

## 3. Worked hints
- "When the team reached the village there was a meeting and everyone decided together": trigger `goonj_meeting`, decision `asserted`; proposer `villagers_collective` only if villagers are the subject of the proposing verb, otherwise `unattributed_passive`.
- A villager who saw the work in a neighbouring village and then approached Goonj: trigger `saw_work_elsewhere`, proposer `named_villager`, `prior_contact` type `saw_work_in_neighbouring_place`.
- A health meeting where a woman suggests a village meeting, and options are listed in order: `options_considered` holds the list, the decision is `narrated`.
- A kitchen garden that appears in every meeting of one programme: trigger `programme_menu`, agenda `programme_menu`, unless the text shows the women choosing it for a stated need (`mixed`).
- "The faces showed joy" is `joy_happiness`, `writer_observed`; "all villagers were very happy" at the end is `writer_asserted`, `boilerplate: true`.
- "Eventually", "for more than two months" or "has already" counts as `observed_later` only when the text gives a time gap or a later event.

## 4. How to work
For each story id in your list: (1) read the story in full; (2) read the prefill; (3) fill a copy of `record_template.json`; (4) write `extract/<ID>.json` (UTF-8, valid JSON, Devanagari unescaped); (5) run `python3 scripts/gate_one.py <ID>` and fix what it reports; repeat until it passes or you have tried three times, then say what is left. Keep evidence spans short and exact; every evidence value must be an exact copy of the story body, not of the `#` header lines. Do not skip fields: use empty lists, `null`, `"none"` or `""` where the text has nothing. If a story is not a field story (a long article, say), still write a record and say so in `_notes`.
